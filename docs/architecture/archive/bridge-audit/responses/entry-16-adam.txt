#### 1. DIMENSIONAL CONSISTENCY

The dimensional consistency of the formula `E_{\min} = k_B T \ln 2` is assessed as follows:

*   **Left-Hand Side (LHS):** `E_{\min}` represents a minimum energy. The physical dimension of energy is `[M L² T⁻²]`.
*   **Right-Hand Side (RHS):** The RHS consists of three terms:
    *   `k_B` (Boltzmann constant): This constant relates temperature to energy. Its dimension is `[energy] [temperature]⁻¹`, or `[M L² T⁻² Θ⁻¹]`.
    *   `T` (absolute temperature): The dimension of temperature is `[Θ]`.
    *   `ln 2` (natural logarithm of 2): This is a dimensionless pure number, with dimension `[1]`.

The dimensional arithmetic for the RHS is:
`[RHS] = [k_B] × [T] × [ln 2] = ([M L² T⁻² Θ⁻¹]) × ([Θ]) × ([1]) = [M L² T⁻²]`

The dimension of the RHS is `[M L² T⁻²]`, which is the dimension of energy.

**Conclusion:** The dimension of the RHS matches the dimension of the LHS. The formula is dimensionally consistent, and it matches the claimed dimensional signature of `[energy]`.

#### 2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS

The formula `E_{\min} = k_B T \ln 2` is the correct, canonical, and universally accepted form of Landauer's principle. It states the minimum energy dissipated as heat into a thermal reservoir at temperature `T` required to erase one bit of information.

This is not a phenomenological ansatz. It is a derived result from statistical mechanics, first proposed by Landauer in the cited 1961 paper. It is considered a foundational result in the thermodynamics of computation and has been experimentally verified to high precision, as correctly noted in the cited literature (e.g., Bérut et al. 2012, Jun et al. 2014). The formula as presented is standard and correct.

#### 3. BRIDGE LEGITIMACY

The equation genuinely connects the two named regimes:

*   **Microscale:** The concept of a "bit" of information is fundamentally microscale. It refers to the state of a system with two distinguishable configurations (e.g., a particle's position in a bistable potential well, a spin orientation). The erasure of a bit is an operation on a single, well-defined microstate, resetting it to a standard state regardless of its initial value.
*   **Emergent:** The quantities on the right-hand side, `E` (energy dissipated as heat) and `T` (temperature), are emergent thermodynamic properties. Temperature is a statistical property of a macroscopic thermal reservoir, and heat is a macroscopic energy transfer process.

The equation `E_{\min} = k_B T \ln 2` provides a direct, quantitative link between a discrete operation on a microscopic information-bearing degree of freedom and the minimum necessary cost in terms of macroscopic, emergent thermodynamic quantities. The "bridge" framing is therefore not a stretch; it is the central physical insight of the principle.
