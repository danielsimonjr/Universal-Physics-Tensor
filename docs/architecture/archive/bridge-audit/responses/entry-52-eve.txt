1. DIMENSIONAL CONSISTENCY
Right-hand side
G → [L³ M⁻¹ T⁻²]
M → [M]
a → [L]
e → dimensionless
c → [L T⁻¹]

Step-by-step:
G M → [L³ M⁻¹ T⁻²] [M] = [L³ T⁻²]
G M / a → [L³ T⁻²] / [L] = [L² T⁻²]
(G M / a) / c² → [L² T⁻²] / ([L T⁻¹]²)
        = [L² T⁻²] / [L² T⁻²] = [1] (dimensionless)

Left-hand side Δφ is an angle; in analytic work angles are treated as dimensionless (radians).
Therefore both sides carry the dimension [1], matching the catalog’s claim.

2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS
The quoted expression
Δφ = 6π G M / (a (1−e²) c²)
for the anomalous perihelion advance per orbit of a test particle in a Schwarzschild field is the standard 1-post-Newtonian (1PN) result found in textbooks (e.g. Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, Eq. 7.48). Alternative but algebraically identical forms replace a by the semi-latus rectum p = a(1−e²) or express the advance per unit time rather than per orbit; none contradict the above. Hence the formula is canonical, not an ad-hoc ansatz.

3. BRIDGE LEGITIMACY
The equation quantifies the leading deviation from a Newtonian Keplerian ellipse that arises when general relativity is treated to 1PN order. It is literally the difference between the Newtonian prediction (zero excess advance) and the GR prediction (non-zero). Thus it operationally bridges the Newtonian and GR regimes; the label is appropriate, not post-hoc.

4. STATUS CALIBRATION
(a) Physics: The equation itself is thoroughly established, repeatedly confirmed (Mercury, binary pulsars, spacecraft tracking).
(b) Framing: Calling it “Einstein 1915 closed-form” and placing it in a “Emergent Spacetime” bridge is a matter of catalog taxonomy, not speculative physics. Nothing in the framing over-claims beyond accepted 1PN validity. Therefore the “established” status is warranted.

5. STRONGEST OBJECTION
The entry omits an explicit statement that the result is a first-order (1PN) approximation valid only for weak-field, slow-motion orbits around an isolated spherically symmetric mass. Presenting it as definitively “closed-form” without that caveat could mislead users who need higher-order corrections (e.g. for stars near Sgr A* or for future sub-µas astrometry).

6. OVERALL VERDICT
The physics is correct; only a clarifying caveat is advisable.
