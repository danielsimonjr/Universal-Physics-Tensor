1. DIMENSIONAL CONSISTENCY
   • Ket vectors |ψ⟩ and |g⟩ live in Hilbert space; by convention they carry no mechanical-dimension units.
   • A unitary operator U(g) is likewise dimensionless.
   • A normalized Haar measure dg is a (dimensionless) probability measure on the group manifold.
   • The integrand therefore has overall dimension [1].
   • An integral of a dimension-[1] quantity over a dimensionless measure is still dimension-[1].
   Conclusion: both sides of |\psi⟩_B = … carry dimension [1], matching the catalog's "dimensional signature [1]".

2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS
   • In the QRF literature the transformation is normally written (Giacomini et al. 2019, Vanrietvelde et al. 2020) as
     |ψ⟩_B = ∫_G dg  U(g) |ψ⟩_A ⊗ |g⟩_frame ,
     possibly with an overall normalization 1/√|G| or a projector ∑_g |g⟩⟨g| ensuring completeness.
   • The entry's equation matches that schematic form but omits (i) an explicit definition of the group G, (ii) the normalization/regularization needed for non-compact groups, and (iii) a projector or inner product that turns the integral into an identity resolution.
   • The extraneous comma "∫ dg , U(g)" is a typographical slip, not physics, but signals the same missing definition of the measure.
   Therefore: it is recognizably the standard ansatz but incomplete and formally ill-defined as written.

3. BRIDGE LEGITIMACY
   The equation maps one quantum description (with frame A) to another quantum description (with frame B). Both regimes are ordinary non-relativistic quantum mechanics; there is no second, distinct physical regime. Labelling the bridge "unknown ↔ unknown" provides no meaningful physical contrast. The bridge framing is therefore artificial.

4. STATUS CALIBRATION
   a) The transformation rule itself is now part of mainstream QRF research and is not speculative in the narrow sense of "unverified physics".
   b) Treating it as a step toward "Emergent Spacetime" or an inter-regime bridge is indeed speculative.
   Overall, the catalog's "speculative" tag is acceptable, but it should be clear that the speculation lies in the interpretation, not in the basic equation.

5. STRONGEST OBJECTION
   The integral is mathematically undefined because the entry never states which group is being integrated over nor how the Haar measure is regularised when that group is non-compact. Without that information the transformation cannot be evaluated, normalized, or even shown to converge.
