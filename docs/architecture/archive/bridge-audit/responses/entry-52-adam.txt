### 1. DIMENSIONAL CONSISTENCY

The dimensional analysis is correct. The claimed signature of `[1]` (dimensionless) is consistent with the formula.

The left-hand side, `Δφ`, is an angle, which is a ratio of lengths (arc length / radius) and is therefore dimensionless. Its dimension is `[1]`.

For the right-hand side (RHS), `6πGM / (a(1−e²)c²)`, the dimensions of the components are:
*   `6π`: A pure number, dimensionless. `[1]`
*   `G` (Gravitational constant): `[G] = M⁻¹ L³ T⁻²`
*   `M` (Mass): `[M]`
*   `a` (Semi-major axis): `[L]`
*   `e` (Eccentricity): A ratio, dimensionless. `[1]`. Therefore, `(1-e²)` is also `[1]`.
*   `c` (Speed of light): `[c] = L T⁻¹`. Therefore, `[c²] = L² T⁻²`.

Combining these for the RHS:
`[RHS] = [G] [M] / ([a] [1-e²] [c²])`
`[RHS] = (M⁻¹ L³ T⁻²) * (M) / ( (L) * (1) * (L² T⁻²) )`
`[RHS] = (M⁻¹⁺¹ L³ T⁻²) / (L¹⁺² T⁻²) `
`[RHS] = (M⁰ L³ T⁻²) / (L³ T⁻²) `
`[RHS] = M⁰ L⁰ T⁰ = [1]`

The dimension of the RHS is `[1]`, which matches the LHS and the claimed signature.

### 2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS

Yes, this is the correct, standard, and canonical form of the equation for the leading-order perihelion advance in General Relativity. It appears in this exact form in standard graduate-level textbooks, such as Carroll's "Spacetime and Geometry" (cited in the entry).

This result is not a phenomenological ansatz. It is derived directly from the geodesic equation for a test mass in the Schwarzschild metric, which describes the spacetime around a static, spherically symmetric massive object. The formula represents the first-order relativistic correction to the otherwise closed elliptical orbits of Newtonian gravity.

### 3. BRIDGE LEGITIMACY

The "bridge" framing is entirely legitimate and accurate. This equation is a textbook example of a bridge between Newtonian and Einsteinian gravity.

The connection is demonstrated by examining the non-relativistic limit. Newtonian gravity can be viewed as the limit of General Relativity where the speed of light `c` is taken to be infinite. In the formula `Δφ = 6πGM / (a(1−e²)c²)`, as `c → ∞`, the denominator becomes infinite, and thus `Δφ → 0`.

A value of `Δφ = 0` corresponds to a perfectly closed, non-precessing elliptical orbit, which is precisely the prediction of Newtonian gravity for an ideal two-body system. The formula therefore explicitly quantifies the deviation from the Newtonian prediction, a deviation that is governed by the finite speed of light (the key postulate of relativity). The presence of both the Newtonian constant `G` and the relativistic constant `c` in a single expression is the hallmark of such a bridge.

### 4. STATUS CALIBRATION

The status "established" is correct.

(a) The equation itself is deeply established. Its derivation from the Schwarzschild solution is a standard result in GR, mathematically undisputed for over a century. More importantly, its prediction has been experimentally verified to extremely high precision. While Mercury's 43 arcseconds/century was the first confirmation, much more stringent tests have been performed on systems like the Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar (PSR B1913+16), where the perihelion advance is much larger (~4.2 degrees per year) and matches the GR prediction to within a fraction of a percent. The cited review by Clifford Will is a definitive source on the modern, high-precision status of this and other GR tests.

(b) The catalog's framing of this as a bridge is also the standard, established interpretation of the formula's physical meaning, as discussed in point (3). There is nothing speculative about this interpretation.

### 5. STRONGEST OBJECTION

The single most serious physics problem with the entry *as written* is a subtle but crucial omission: it fails to explicitly connect the quantitative output of the formula (radians per orbit) with the famous observational value it cites (arcseconds per century).

The context correctly states the formula is for precession "per orbit" and that the observed value for Mercury is "~43 arcsec/century". However, a user of the catalog could apply the formula to Mercury's parameters and be confused by the result.

*   The formula yields `Δφ` in radians per orbit, a very small dimensionless number (~5.02 x 10⁻⁷ for Mercury).
*   The observation is a rate in units of angular distance per unit time.

To bridge this gap, one must perform a unit conversion using Mercury's orbital period:
1.  Calculate `Δφ` in radians/orbit from the formula.
2.  Convert radians to arcseconds (1 radian ≈ 206,265 arcseconds).
3.  Calculate the number of orbits Mercury completes per century (100 years / ~88 days/orbit ≈ 415.2 orbits/century).
4.  Multiply these values: (rad/orbit) × (arcsec/rad) × (orbits/century) = arcsec/century.

Failing to state or show this conversion is a significant pedagogical omission. It leaves the user with a formula and a famous number, implying a connection but not demonstrating it, which can lead to confusion about the formula's direct applicability.
