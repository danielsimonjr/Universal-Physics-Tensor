1. DIMENSIONAL CONSISTENCY
Left-hand side: ΔF is a Helmholtz free–energy difference ⇒ dimension [energy].

Right-hand side: −kB T ln⟨exp( −W / (kB T) )⟩

• W has dimension [energy].
• kB T also has dimension [energy].
• Therefore W/(kB T) is dimensionless.
• exp( … ) is dimensionless; its ensemble average ⟨…⟩ is likewise dimensionless.
• ln of a dimensionless number is dimensionless.
• kB T in front supplies the factor of [energy].

Thus RHS has dimension [energy], matching LHS and the catalog's "[energy]" claim.

2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS
The displayed formula
ΔF = −kB T ln⟨exp(−W/(kB T))⟩
is exactly the standard Jarzynski equality as first written in Phys. Rev. Lett. 78, 2690 (1997) and repeated unaltered in the 1997 Phys. Rev. E companion paper. No deviations or missing factors are present. Therefore it is not a phenomenological ansatz; it is an established, rigorously derived result in nonequilibrium statistical mechanics.

3. BRIDGE LEGITIMACY
A "bridge equation" is supposed to connect two distinct physical regimes. The entry lists the regimes as "unknown <-> unknown", i.e. none are specified. The bare Jarzynski equality relates an equilibrium free-energy difference to an average over explicitly non-equilibrium work trajectories, but both sides live squarely within classical (or quantum) statistical mechanics at fixed temperature; there is no cross-disciplinary bridge (e.g. quantum↔classical, relativistic↔non-relativistic). Hence, the bridge framing is at best rhetorical and at worst nonexistent for the equation actually kept in the catalog. The dropped curved-spacetime extension, if it had been retained and justified, might have constituted a statistical-mechanics ↔ general-relativity bridge, but that term has been removed.

4. STATUS CALIBRATION
(a) Physics of the equation: firmly established.
(b) Catalog framing (link to gravity; "unknown↔unknown" regimes): speculative at best.
Therefore labeling the whole entry "speculative" is defensible only because of the unsupported bridge interpretation, not because of the Jarzynski equality itself. A more precise catalog should tag the equality itself as "established" and tag the gravitational extension, presently excised, as "highly speculative".

5. STRONGEST OBJECTION
The catalog now retains only the canonical Jarzynski equality yet still lists the entry as a speculative "bridge" without specifying which two regimes are bridged. The bridge claim is therefore unsubstantiated: with the gravity term removed, nothing in the entry actually crosses disciplinary boundaries. That mismatch between content and stated purpose is the most serious flaw.
