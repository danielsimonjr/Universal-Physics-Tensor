1. DIMENSIONAL CONSISTENCY
   • Take Newtonian force: F_N has dimension [M L T⁻²].
   • Define z = F_N /(m a₀).  Dimensions of numerator: [M L T⁻²]; denominator m a₀: [M]·[L T⁻²] = [M L T⁻²].  Hence z is dimensionless.
   • ν(z) is an algebraic function of a dimensionless variable, so ν is itself dimensionless.
   • RHS of the bridge equation: F_N·ν(z) → [M L T⁻²]·(dimensionless) = [M L T⁻²] = [force].
   • LHS F is labelled “force”.
   Therefore both sides carry the same dimension; the claimed dimensional signature [force] is satisfied.

2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS
   • In MOND literature two equivalent forms are common:
     (A) μ(a/a₀) a = a_N, with μ(x)=x/√(1+x²) (“simple” form, Milgrom 1983).
     (B) a = ν(a_N/a₀) a_N, where ν(y) is the “inverse-μ” function
        ν(y)=½+½√(1+4/y²)⁠^{1/2}  → algebraically identical to the ν(z) given here.
   • The entry writes the force version F = F_N ν(z).  Because a = F/m, rewriting (B) gives exactly this force form, so the equation is a standard MOND interpolation written in force variables.
   • HOWEVER, the heading “Entropic Gravity Correction Term” and the references to Verlinde (2016/2017) suggest a derivation from emergent/entropic gravity.  Verlinde’s 2017 SciPost paper does not yield Milgrom’s simple ν(y); it predicts a different r-dependent addition that only reproduces MOND in the asymptotic regime.  Thus, while the formula is canonical inside phenomenological MOND, it is not the canonical form of Verlinde’s entropic-gravity result.
   • Conclusion: the formula is a phenomenological ansatz adopted from Milgrom, not a derived result of Verlinde’s theory.

3. BRIDGE LEGITIMACY
   • The catalog lists the bridged regimes as “unknown <-> unknown”.  In practice the equation tries to connect Newtonian gravity (well-known) to galaxy-scale dynamical anomalies (usually attributed either to dark matter or to modified gravity).
   • Because neither side of that connection is unambiguously identified here—and the underlying mechanism is only conjectured—the “bridge” is more of a phenomenological patch than a demonstrable link between two well-defined theoretical regimes.  The bridge framing is therefore weak at best.

4. STATUS CALIBRATION
   (a) The equation itself is established in the sense of being the standard “simple-ν” MOND interpolation; it has been in continuous use since 1983.
   (b) The interpretation that this ν(z) comes from Verlinde’s entropic gravity, or that it provides a fundamental explanation for galaxy dynamics without dark matter, is unconfirmed and controversial.
   Therefore “speculative” is an appropriate catalog label—correct for the framing, even though the empirical formula is longstanding.

5. STRONGEST OBJECTION
   The entry conflates two distinct constructs: Milgrom’s empirical MOND interpolation (which the written formula actually is) and Verlinde’s emergent-gravity proposal (which yields a different functional dependence).  Presenting the Milgrom ν(z) as a “Verlinde entropic gravity correction” misrepresents the physics origin and can mislead users about its theoretical status.

6. OVERALL VERDICT
   The dimensional check passes and the formula matches the standard MOND interpolation, but the conceptual framing is seriously muddled—claiming a Verlinde derivation that it does not possess and offering no clear “bridge” between defined regimes.  That is more than a minor editorial issue.
