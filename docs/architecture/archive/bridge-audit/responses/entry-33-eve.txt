2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS
• Standard Hertz–Millis finite-temperature scaling at the quantum critical coupling g = gc (the “quantum–critical trajectory”) reads
 ξ(T) ∝ T–1/z.
The static exponent ν does NOT appear; it governs the r ≡ g–gc dependence at T = 0, whereas the temperature dependence along r = 0 is controlled solely by the dynamic exponent z.  This form is given explicitly, for example, in:
 – Sondhi et al., Rev. Mod. Phys. 69, 315 (1997), Eq. (2.18)
 – Sachdev, Quantum Phase Transitions, 2nd ed., Sec. 11.1
• The entry’s formula ξ ∼ T–ν/z therefore deviates from the canonical result by an unnecessary factor ν in the exponent.  It is not the standard form.
• Because the formula disagrees with the literature, it should be classified as a phenomenological ansatz, not an “established” Hertz–Millis expression.

3. BRIDGE LEGITIMACY
The intended bridge is the well-known mapping of a d-dimensional quantum critical point to a (d+z)-dimensional classical critical point (quantum ↔ classical).  That is a legitimate conceptual bridge.  However the metadata says “unknown ↔ unknown”, so the entry fails to specify the two regimes it purports to connect.  The physics idea is sound, the catalog wording is incomplete.

4. STATUS CALIBRATION
(a) Physics of the Hertz–Millis mapping itself: established.
(b) The specific equation given in the entry: not established (incorrect exponent).
Given that mismatch, “speculative” is actually too generous; the equation is wrong, not merely speculative.  A better label would be “invalid” (for this formula) or the entry should be corrected to the canonical ξ ∼ T–1/z form and relabelled “established”.

5. STRONGEST OBJECTION
The exponent –ν/z is incorrect for the finite-temperature correlation length at a quantum critical point; the correct Hertz–Millis scaling is –1/z.  Including ν conflates temperature scaling with tuning-parameter scaling and misrepresents the underlying renormalization-group result.
