The formula is internally consistent, with both sides having the dimension of energy density, `[M]⁴`.

### 2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS

The formula is an algebraically valid, if slightly unconventional, representation of the canonical potential in this class of models.

The standard phenomenological potential for a minimal composite Higgs model is typically written in the form:
`V(h) = c₁ f⁴ sin²(h/f) + c₂ f⁴ sin⁴(h/f)`
where `c₁` and `c₂` are dimensionless coefficients.

The entry's formula is:
`V(h) = -α f⁴ sin²(h/f) + β f⁴ [sin⁴(h/f) - sin²(h/f)cos²(h/f)]`

Let's simplify the term in the square brackets using the identity `cos²(x) = 1 - sin²(x)`:
`sin⁴(h/f) - sin²(h/f)[1 - sin²(h/f)]`
`= sin⁴(h/f) - sin²(h/f) + sin⁴(h/f)`
`= 2 sin⁴(h/f) - sin²(h/f)`

Substituting this back into the potential gives:
`V(h) = -α f⁴ sin²(h/f) + β f⁴ [2 sin⁴(h/f) - sin²(h/f)]`
`V(h) = -(α + β) f⁴ sin²(h/f) + 2β f⁴ sin⁴(h/f)`

This is precisely the canonical form with `c₁ = -(α + β)` and `c₂ = 2β`. So, while the expression is written in a slightly obfuscated manner, it is physically and mathematically equivalent to the standard form.

It is crucial to note that this potential is a **phenomenological ansatz**. It is part of an effective field theory (EFT) description. The coefficients (`α`, `β`) are not derived from first principles but are parameters that would, in principle, be determined by the unknown underlying strong dynamics (the UV completion).
