1. DIMENSIONAL CONSISTENCY
λ₀ is given as 10⁻¹⁶ s⁻¹ → dimension [T⁻¹].
m and m₀ are both masses → each has dimension [M].
m/m₀ is therefore dimensionless.

Right-hand side: [λ₀]·(dimensionless) = [T⁻¹].
Left-hand side λ_GRW(m) is a rate, so presumed [T⁻¹].
Result: both sides carry the frequency dimension; the claimed signature “[frequency]” is satisfied.

2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS
• In the original GRW (1986) model the collapse rate for a *single* nucleon is λ₀ ≈ 10⁻¹⁶ s⁻¹.
• Pearle’s CSL reformulation (1989) and Ghirardi-Pearle-Rimini (1990) introduce the “amplification mechanism”: for a system whose total (rest) mass is m, the rate scales linearly with mass,
 λ(m) = (m/m₀) λ₀, with m₀ the nucleon mass.

That is exactly the equation written in BE-48. Nothing is missing or altered; it is the standard scalar version quoted in the review by Bassi & Ghirardi (Phys. Rep. 379 §2.1).
However, it is an *axiom* of the GRW/CSL phenomenology, not a result derived from more fundamental theory.

3. BRIDGE LEGITIMACY
UPT marks the bridge “unknown ↔ unknown”. In practice the GRW/CSL rate is intended to interpolate between
• the microscopic quantum regime (linear Schrödinger evolution) and
• a putative macroscopic classical regime produced by objective collapse.

Whether that truly *connects* those regimes is a matter of interpretation; the equation itself is simply a parameter choice inside one speculative modification of quantum mechanics. By itself the scalar rate does not demonstrably link two independently established theories, so the “bridge” label is, at best, a loose, post-hoc framing.

4. STATUS CALIBRATION
(a) The equation λ(m)=λ₀ (m/m₀) is well-established *within* the GRW/CSL literature; there is no controversy about its form.
(b) The physical *validity* of GRW/CSL as a description of nature is unconfirmed and strongly constrained experimentally. Hence calling the *entry* “speculative” is appropriate. The catalog correctly separates the solid internal formula from the uncertain physical interpretation.

5. STRONGEST OBJECTION
The entry isolates the rate constant but omits the second essential CSL parameter, the localization length r_C ≈ 10⁻⁷ m. Without r_C the physical predictions (e.g., decoherence rates, energy increase) are under-determined; experimental bounds on λ₀ are always quoted in the (λ₀, r_C) plane. Presenting λ(m) alone therefore gives an incomplete—and potentially misleading—picture of the model’s empirical status.
