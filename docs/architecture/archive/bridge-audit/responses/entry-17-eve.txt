1. DIMENSIONAL CONSISTENCY

Base units:
 length L, mass M, time T;  c has units L T⁻¹;  G has units L³ M⁻¹ T⁻².

Einstein part

a) Left-hand side
 R_{μν}, R  → curvature  → L⁻²
 g_{μν} is dimensionless.
 So every term on the LHS has unit L⁻².

b) Right-hand side
 Stress–energy tensor: T_{μν} ~ (energy density) → M L⁻¹ T⁻²
 Prefactor: G/c⁴ → (L³ M⁻¹ T⁻²)/(L⁴ T⁻⁴) = L⁻¹ M⁻¹ T²

 (G/c⁴) T_{μν} → (L⁻¹ M⁻¹ T²)(M L⁻¹ T⁻²)=L⁻²

Hence the Einstein equation is dimensionally consistent and carries the single dimension
 [L⁻²].

Torsion–spin part

c) Torsion tensor T^{λ}{}_{μν} is built from connection coefficients; ∂/∂x → L⁻¹, so [T]=L⁻¹.

d) Spin density S^{λ}{}_{μν}: angular-momentum per volume.
 Angular momentum J → M L² T⁻¹.
 Divide by volume L³ ⇒ M L⁻¹ T⁻¹.

 Again G/c⁴ → L⁻¹ M⁻¹ T².
 (G/c⁴) S → (L⁻¹ M⁻¹ T²)(M L⁻¹ T⁻¹)=L⁻² T¹.

So the two sides of the torsion equation do NOT match dimensionally unless an extra factor of (1/c) is hidden in the definition of S or one works in units c=1.  As written, their dimensions differ by L⁻¹ T⁻¹.

Claimed dimensional signature in the entry: [L⁻² M² T⁻²].
That does not match any side of either equation.  Verdict: the stated signature is wrong.

2. CANONICAL CORRECTNESS

• Einstein piece: R_{μν} – ½R g_{μν} + Λ g_{μν}= (8πG/c⁴) T_{μν} is exactly the standard Einstein equation.

• Torsion piece:  T^{λ}{}_{μν} = (8πG/c⁴) S^{λ}{}_{μν} is the usual algebraic relation in Einstein–Cartan theory (sometimes written with κ≡8πG/c⁴).

So the functional form is canonical, but the dimensional mismatch noted above means one must be in units with c=1 and the spin density normalized appropriately; that caveat should be stated.

3. BRIDGE LEGITIMACY

Einstein–Cartan theory links spacetime geometry (torsion/curvature) to intrinsic spin of matter.  It does NOT relate electromagnetism to gravity.  Since the entry’s “field-A ↔ field-B” is now an empty placeholder, the bridge claim is unclear.  If the authors intend “gravity ↔ quantum spin” the bridge is legitimate; if they still imply any EM connection it is not.
