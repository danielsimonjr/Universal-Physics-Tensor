## Overall assessment

UPT is an unusually epistemically disciplined project, and that
discipline makes its central weakness visible: **the engineering is far
ahead of the science.**

What is genuinely strong:

- **Epistemic honesty.** The Part-I framing commitment ("Π is a catalog,
  not a tensor; 'tensor' is brand"), the status taxonomy with no
  unearned promotions (8 established · 33 speculative · 3
  highly-speculative), honest negative results (PC-1 refuted and
  documented), and the explicit "NOT claiming to have unified physics"
  posture. This is rare and is the project's best asset.
- **Engineering discipline.** 2126 tests, bidirectional spec↔code drift
  guards, regression pins on past bugs (ricci slot, prefactor
  reconciliations), and an adversarial-review loop that has caught real
  defects.
- **Real numerics.** The GL4 symplectic integrator validated against
  Mercury's perihelion (relErr 1.8×10⁻⁷) and Shapiro delay (~2×10⁻⁸) is
  legitimate numerical relativity. The dimensional AST validator with
  machine-checked round-trips across 42 encoded bridges is the project's
  most defensible scientific artifact.

The unanswered question is the one the README's own Vision section
poses: what does representing all of physics in a single indexed object
*buy*?

## The core object: from rank-6 container to typed quantity graph

*(Design-level resolution of G-1 and G-2. The right object has been
hiding in Part-IX all along: bridges are morphisms, so the core object
should be the structure they generate.)*

### Why the rank-6 tensor is the wrong shape

1. **Bridges are relational; a tensor is positional.** A cell in Π lives
   *at* a 6-tuple of labels, but every bridge connects *two or more*
   regimes — which is why `bridges: ['quantum', 'cosmological']` exists
   as a field bolted onto each catalog entry. The connecting structure
   was never in the tensor; it was in metadata the tensor cannot
   express.
2. **The tensor's algebra was amputated at birth.** The Part-I framing
   commitment had to disavow inner products, traces, norms, and
   contraction on Π — every operation that makes a tensor a tensor. An
   object whose entire surviving operation set is "look up by index" is
   an index, not a unifier.
3. **Emergence never fit.** E is an inherently *n*-ary relation
   ("this phenomenon arises from the interaction of these inputs"); a
   rank-6 container has no slot for that.

### The replacement

A **typed, attributed multigraph of physical quantities, whose edges are
bridge equations, with partial composition and a dimension functor** —
in spec language, a symmetric monoidal category of relations; in
implementation language, three TypeScript interfaces and one function.

- **Objects = quantities.** `(name, Dimension, regime-attributes)`. The
  six axes don't die; they are demoted from container dimensions to
  *attributes on objects* — which is what they always behaved as.
- **Morphisms = bridges.** Edges from source quantities (plural — n-ary
  inputs are first-class, which is how Emergence finally fits) to a
  target quantity, carrying the formula AST, a **validity domain**, a
  status/confidence, and an evaluator. Morphisms are *relations with
  domains*, not total functions: bridges are approximate and
  regime-bounded, so composition must be partial.
- **Composition = Part-IX promoted.** `compose(b1, b2)` is defined when
  b1's output type matches one of b2's inputs *and the validity domains
  intersect*. Part-IX's numerical cascade stops being a research
  appendix and becomes *the* defining operation of the framework.
- **The dimension functor is already built.** SI dimensions form an
  abelian group (ℤ⁷ under exponent addition); dimensional consistency of
  every bridge is precisely the statement that assigning each quantity
  its dimension is a structure-preserving map into that group. That map
  is `src/dimensional/` — the 42 encoded round-trips are functoriality
  checks. The most speculative-sounding part of the redesign is the part
  that is already implemented and tested.

### What this buys, gap by gap

- **G-1 dissolves:** `compose()` either type-checks (dimensions multiply
  correctly, domains overlap) and yields a candidate derived relation,
  or fails with a reason. Deriving a literature-confirmed relation
  becomes a graph-path query.
- **G-2 gets its membership criterion for free:** *a bridge is a
  morphism whose source and target quantities differ in regime
  attributes.* The BE-42/BE-29 NOT-A-BRIDGE disputes become decidable by
  inspecting endpoints rather than by taste.
- **G-3 gets a carrier:** edges carry uncertainties, and uncertainty
  *propagates through composition*.
- **Part-I invariant 4 (correspondence) stops being vacuous:** "ℏ→0
  recovers the classical equation" becomes "these two paths through the
  graph agree on the overlap of their validity domains" — checkable per
  pair.

### Design notes accumulated during the review

- **Quantity identification is a feature, not a chore.** The same
  physical quantity appears across bridges under different symbols —
  Part-I's polyvalent-symbol glossary is direct evidence the problem
  already exists. Building the graph forces explicit "this ξ IS that ξ /
  is NOT that ξ" decisions, each of which is a small, reviewable piece
  of physics judgment. Expect the quantity-node inventory to be the
  slowest, most valuable part of the migration.
- **Confidence algebra.** Status should become a composition rule, not
  just a label: `confidence(b2 ∘ b1) = min(confidence(b1),
  confidence(b2))` (composing speculative with established yields
  speculative). This makes the status taxonomy load-bearing for the
  first time and prevents derived relations from silently inheriting
  unearned credibility.
- **The six axes are unevenly load-bearing — let attributes be sparse.**
  Scale and force do real work in the catalog today; symmetry and
  information sometimes; the dimension axis is redundant with
  `dimensional_signature`; topology is nearly unused. The graph should
  not force all six attributes on every quantity. Drop the pretense of a
  full Cartesian product; record what is known.
- **Rule out the heavier alternatives for now.** A sheaf over regime
  space elegantly captures "theories agreeing on overlaps" but requires
  a topology on regime space that cannot currently be justified —
  revisit if composition works. A knowledge graph *without* composition
  laws is just the catalog with arrows drawn on it. And keep category
  theory as the spec language (a rewritten Part-I), not the
  implementation — the code is `Quantity`, `Bridge`, `compose()`.

### Migration path (incremental, not a rewrite)

1. Catalog rows → edge declarations (mechanical; `bridges:` tuples are
   already proto-endpoints).
2. v0.7 `UniversalIndex`/`Axes` machinery → the attribute typing layer
   (survives nearly intact).
3. Flux rules → graph well-formedness conditions; `populatedCells()` →
   coverage query. Π survives as a *view* over the graph, and per the
   framing commitment "Universal Physics Tensor" was already declared a
   brand label, so the name keeps.
4. **First milestone:** represent the Part-IX C1–C5 calibration set as
   typed edges and make `compose()` reproduce one known relation
   end-to-end. This doubles as Part-IX Phase B — the composition MVP and
   the graph pilot are the same work item.

**Sequencing recommendation:** do NOT schedule a big-bang migration.
v0.8.0 ships the C1–C5 composition MVP on graph-lite structures
(`Quantity`/`Bridge`/`compose()` alongside the existing catalog, not
replacing it); v0.9.0 migrates the catalog itself if and only if the MVP
derives something real. The MVP is falsifiable; the migration is not.

## Major gaps (ranked)

### G-1. The central object doesn't do anything yet

Π has six axes, but no computation uses the six-axis structure. All
computational value lives in individual bridge evaluators and the GR
pipeline — a flat list of 44 bridges would deliver identical results
today. The v0.7 flux rules and Cell machinery are bookkeeping over the
structure, not physics from it.

**Resolution path:** the core-object redesign above; concretely the
C1–C5 composition MVP (= Part-IX Phase B = graph pilot).

### G-2. The bridge ontology is unprincipled

The catalog is a curated reading list more than a defined class of
objects. The physics audit flagged ~9 entries as NOT-A-BRIDGE and
renamed 17; the BE-42 and BE-29 reversals are still parked. Many entries
are famous single-domain equations relabeled as bridges.

**Resolution path:** adopt the graph-native membership criterion (a
bridge is an edge whose endpoint quantities differ in regime
attributes); re-adjudicate the full catalog against it, folding in the
deferred BE-42/BE-29 decisions. A 30-bridge catalog with a principled
definition beats a 44-bridge catalog without one.

### G-3. Validation is asymmetric: reproduction, not confrontation

Everything validated so far reproduces known closed-form results — which
validates the *machinery*, not the *bridges*. Part-IV's experimental
roadmap is honest but aspirational (2035–2050+). Bridges that could meet
real data now: BE-36's GW170817 graviton-speed bound, BE-21/BE-23
condensed-matter scalings.

**Resolution path:** real-data validation pipeline, first target
GW170817 → BE-36 (the bound is already encoded as a dimensionless
ratio). Edge-level uncertainties from the redesign give the result a
home.

### G-4. The core spec dilutes credibility

Parts I–VI still carry large speculative essays (Part-V medical device
specifications and existential risk; Part-VI cosmic engineering),
caveated but present. Meanwhile the spec inverted: the real algorithms
(GL4, lowering, the validator) are spec'd only in supplements
VII/VIII/X, while the core parts describe pseudocode none of which is
implemented.

**Resolution path:** relocate the application/risk essays into a
separate `docs/essays/` companion; let Parts I–VI shrink toward
foundation + catalog + what is actually built. If the core-object
redesign proceeds, Part-I gets rewritten around the quantity graph and
this relegation should happen in the same pass.

### G-5. No on-ramp for the collaborators the README asks for

No `CONTRIBUTING.md`, no per-bridge "what would falsify this" statement,
no arXiv/Zenodo presence or DOI to cite. A physicist arriving today has
no bounded task to pick up.

**Resolution path:** CONTRIBUTING.md listing 5–10 concrete review tasks
(e.g., "check BE-23's Planckian-dissipation encoding against Hartnoll
2015"; "adjudicate the BE-42 NOT-A-BRIDGE dispute — both arguments are
in BRIDGE-PHYSICS-AUDIT-v2"). The quantity-identification work from the
redesign is itself a rich source of bounded physicist tasks. Consider a
Zenodo DOI for citability.

### G-6. No CI

Verified 2026-06-10: no `.github/workflows/`. All 2126 tests run only
when someone runs them. A GitHub Actions gate (test + tsc + drift
guards) is cheap, makes the green badge public, mechanically protects
against spec↔code drift, gives a Linux test environment for free
(today's suite runs only on the Windows dev box with its 3–5 min
cold-start tax), and complements the LLM-only Adam+Eve loop, whose
ceiling is documented (Eve fabrication history; two plan bugs that
slipped to TDD).

### G-7. Smaller debts (mostly already in existing ledgers)

- Orphaned `src/bridges/equations/be-25-orch-or.ts` beside the IIT
  replacement (archive-or-delete deferred from v0.7.3 queue).
- `KleinGordonEquationNode` has no numerical evaluator.
- Kretschmann O(4⁸) contraction cost (P-6, deferred).
- MathTS optional-peer chain fragility (typed-function EOVERRIDE class).
- `describe.skipIf` debt: one remaining gate in
  `tests/diff/bridge-gradient.test.ts`.

### G-8. Validity domains are prose, not predicates

Every bridge has a domain of validity — "b ≫ r_s", "0 ≤ e < 1",
"ρ ≪ σ", "T near T_c" — but today these live in `notes:` strings and
ad-hoc evaluator input guards. Nothing can ask "do these two bridges
overlap?", which is exactly the question composition (G-1) and the
correspondence-principle check both need. **Resolution path:** promote
validity domains to a first-class machine-checkable field
(`domain: Predicate` on each bridge), starting with the C1–C5 set. This
is a prerequisite for the composition MVP, not a nice-to-have.

### G-9. SI-unit internals invite catastrophic cancellation

The c²-cancellation class of bugs has now bitten at least three times
(v0.5.1 Minkowski tests, the Schwarzschild fixture migration, the
4th-order-pderiv mitigation): any metric carrying c² ≈ 9×10¹⁶ on g_tt
puts 2nd-order finite differences at risk, and the mitigation so far has
been per-site (opt-in `order: 4`, unitless fixtures). **Resolution
path:** a units-normalization layer — geometrized/natural units
internally, SI conversion only at API boundaries — would retire the
entire bug class instead of patching instances. Medium effort, high
payoff for every future GR-adjacent bridge.

### G-10. The test suite is broad but not adversarial about algebra

2126 tests are overwhelmingly example-based unit/structural pins.
The dimensional calculus is a perfect target for property-based testing
(e.g. fast-check): `dim(a·b) = dim(a)+dim(b)`,
`dim(a/b) = dim(a)−dim(b)`, exponent round-trips, free-index-set
algebra under contraction, `compose()` associativity once it exists.
A few dozen property tests would cover input space the current suite
samples pointwise — and the composition MVP should be born with them.

## Top picks for v0.8.0

1. **C1–C5 composition MVP** — `Quantity`/`Bridge`/`compose()` graph-lite
   structures beside the existing catalog; one literature-confirmed
   composed relation end-to-end. Subsumes Part-IX Phase B and pilots the
   core-object redesign. Prerequisite: G-8 validity-domain predicates
   for the five calibration bridges. (G-1, G-2, G-8)
2. **Wire one real dataset to one bridge.** GW170817 → BE-36. (G-3)
3. **Bridge membership criterion + catalog re-adjudication**, resolving
   the deferred BE-42/BE-29 NOT-A-BRIDGE decisions — using the
   graph-native criterion so the adjudication and the redesign agree.
   (G-2)

Supporting (cheap, high leverage, can land any time): CI workflow (G-6),
CONTRIBUTING.md (G-5), property-based tests for the dimensional algebra
(G-10).

Deferrable to v0.9+: catalog→graph migration (gated on MVP success),
units-normalization layer (G-9), core-spec essay relegation + Part-I
rewrite (G-4), G-7 items.

## Addendum: further propositions (2026-06-10, second pass)

### P-1. Encode the diagonal — the laws are missing from their own framework

BE-1–10 (Schrödinger, Newton, Maxwell, Einstein, Standard Model) are
"implicit diagonal laws, not individually catalogued." But composition
chains almost always need a *law* as connective tissue — you get from a
quantum quantity to a thermal one *through* a law, not just through
other bridges (e.g., any path involving Hawking temperature passes
through both GR and thermodynamics proper). The best-established physics
in the entire project is currently absent from its data model. The
graph MVP should include at least the 2–3 diagonal laws the C1–C5
calibration set touches, encoded with the same machinery as bridges
(they are just edges whose endpoints share regime attributes — the
complement of the bridge criterion, which is itself a nice consistency
check on the criterion).

### P-2. Catalog-as-data: emit a versioned JSON artifact

`BRIDGE_EQUATIONS` lives in TypeScript source, so a physicist cannot
review or amend the catalog without reading TS. Generate a validated
JSON artifact (catalog + schema, own version number, emitted at build)
as the canonical review surface. PRs from domain experts then touch
data, not code; the drift guards already in place verify the round
trip. Direct enabler for G-5, and it future-proofs the catalog against
the graph migration (edges serialize the same way).

### P-3. Pre-register composition targets

To keep the composition MVP honest, name the relation it intends to
derive *before* building it — in the Phase B design doc, with the
literature citation it will be checked against. Candidates worth
shortlisting: BE-51 + BE-52 share the weak-field Schwarzschild metric
(their ratio is a parameter-free consistency relation); BE-42 Hawking
temperature composed with thermodynamic bridges; BE-12 thermal de
Broglie + BE-16 Landauer. A derivation chosen after the machinery works
proves much less than one predicted before.

### P-4. Keep a negative catalog

The NOT-A-BRIDGE adjudications (9 flagged, BE-42/BE-29 contested) are
scientific output — record them as first-class data (`rejected:` set
with reasons and citations) rather than as audit-doc prose. Prevents
re-litigation, documents the membership criterion by counterexample,
and is honest in exactly the way this project's brand demands. (The
BE-25 archive-or-delete question from the v0.7.3 queue is the same
decision — resolve both with one policy.)

### P-5. Formalize the status-promotion protocol

The project's review loop is LLM-only (Adam+Eve, plus sessions like
this one), and its own ledgers document the failure modes (Eve
fabrication history, plan bugs reaching TDD). Make explicit what is
already implicit culture: **LLM consensus is never sufficient to
promote a bridge's status.** Promotion to `established` requires a
human-verifiable literature anchor; any future promotion driven by
composition or data-confrontation results requires the underlying
dataset or derivation to be independently re-runnable. One paragraph in
Part-VI (governance) — cheap to write, and it inoculates the project
against the most likely external criticism of its methodology.

## Closing note

The project's signature virtue is that it never claims more than it
has. The next phase should be about *having more*: one derivation, one
dataset, one definition. The redesign above is in service of exactly
those three — the graph is not the goal; the first composed,
literature-confirmed relation is.
