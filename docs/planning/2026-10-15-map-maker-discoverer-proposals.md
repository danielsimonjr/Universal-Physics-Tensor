# Twenty ways to make UPT a better bridge-equations map maker and discoverer

Design and intent only. Status, counts and dates are in `NOTES.md`, `ACTIVE.md` and `CHANGELOG.md`. Each proposal names what it changes, the MathTS lever where one exists, the files, the test that proves it, and the gain. They come from four read-only surveys of `src/` on the day the Lean move and the MathTS routing merged, and from the program's own recorded results: the discovery funnel is a rigorous coincidence-rejector (zero genuine identifications over eight adjudications), the catalog graph is a star with a long isolated tail, link prediction by the typed graph does not beat word overlap, and a claim's text is retrieved far better by an embedding than by typed search. The proposals do not try to make the funnel say yes more often. They make its no sharper, its maps truer, and its measurements carry uncertainties.

## A. Decide more, and exactly

**1. Identifiability by rank in log space.** A monomial relation `y = C Π xᵢ^{aᵢ}` is one linear row `log y − Σ aᵢ log xᵢ = log C`. Stack the rows of every monomial edge with unit rows for the known quantities and ask MathTS `rationalNullspace` for the null space: a quantity is determined exactly when its column vanishes in every basis vector, and the surplus `rows − rank` is the number of independent consistency checks. Today `identifiability.ts` runs a forward closure only (an edge fires when all its sources are known), so no law is ever used in reverse and restated monomials are counted twice (the file says so). Files: `composition/identifiability.ts`, its callers in `retrodiction.ts` and `discovery.ts`. Test: a three-quantity chain where the anchor sits at the END is determined; a restatement adds no check. Gain: `upt discover`, `ground` and `recover` gain the laws in reverse, and the over-count becomes a count.

**2. An algebraic-equivalence step behind the normal-form hash.** `normalForm` flattens products only, so `a/(b/c)` and `a·c/b`, `a·a` and `a²`, hash differently and a chain that recovers a known law can be filed as provisional. On a hash miss with equal dimension, ask MathTS `simplify` whether `A/B` reduces to a constant node (the equivalence up to a prefactor the catalog cares about). Files: `canonical/structural.ts`, `composition/chain-match.ts`, `proposed-bridges.ts` dedup, `probe/corpus.ts`. Test: the two quotient forms are one confirmation; `a+b` against `a·b` is not. Gain: fewer false "provisional" chains and false novelty lines. MathTS `symbolicEqual` is not used until the release that fixes it reaches the dependency range (it passed an empty rule list and compared every pair false).

**3. Clean prefactors from a rule, not a list.** The audit decides `grounded` against `empirical` by a list of seventeen constants at 1e-3. Replace the list with a search over `p/(πᵃ ln2ᵇ)` for small `a`, `b`, rationalised by core `Fraction` with a bounded denominator and accepted only within the same 1e-9 the fit uses; print the exact form (`×9/8`, `×π/ln 2`). Files: `composition/bridge-analysis.ts`. Test: be-80's 9/8 and be-84's π/ln 2 are recognised; the Stefan–Boltzmann and Wien prefactors are not. Gain: a stated rule, and two bridges the list mislabelled.

**4. Say which exponent differs.** When a user formula does not match the canonical monomial, `derive` and `compareWithCanonical` only say "differs in form". Sample a full-rank log design (a base point plus one perturbation per variable), fit `log|ratio|` by MathTS `ols`, rationalise the exponents, and report `yours/canonical ∝ speed⁻¹·mass^½` with `R²`; below 1 the difference is not a monomial. Files: `composition/canonical-compare.ts`, `cli/commands/derive.ts`. Test: `v²/2` against `m v²/2` reports `mass⁻¹`. Gain: a diagnosis instead of a verdict.

**5. Exact rational exponents in proposals and shapes.** `proposed-bridges.ts` and `formula-shape.ts` carry exponents as doubles, so solving for a cubed leaf writes `0.6666666666666666` into a Part XI proposal and an exact zero test can miss a cancellation. Carry core `Fraction` and render the literal from it. Test: solving `y = x³` for `x` prints `^(1/3)`; two proposals that differ by rounding dedupe to one. Gain: exact, readable proposals that agree with Buckingham's exact exponents.

**6. One owner for "a constant times that one".** Seven copies decide whether two formulas agree up to a constant, with four sample-point schemes, three statistics and two tolerances, so `derive`, `audit`, `explain`, `map --equation`, `recover` and the probe corpus can answer the same question differently. One `fixed-points` module (sample points, the ratio statistic, the tolerance) with each caller's parameters passed explicitly. Test: the seven verdicts on the catalog are unchanged by the move. Gain: one answer.

**7. One owner for the AST leaf walk and the literal rule.** Thirteen walks over `ExprNode` leaves, six rules for "is this name a number" (one accepts `0x2`, one rejects `1e3`, several accept the empty string), and three rules for "is this a registered constant" (`in` on a plain object is true for `constructor`). One `dimensional/ast-leaves.ts` with the walker, the literal rule and `isRegisteredConstant`. Test: each old rule's edge spellings against the one rule. Gain: the funnel, the probe and the canonical layer read a formula the same way.

## B. Measure with uncertainties

**8. Power-law discovery in the probe.** The native generator emits only the unique Buckingham monomial; when π-groups remain nothing is searched. Fit `log(y/base) = log C + Σ aₖ log πₖ` by MathTS `ols`, rationalise an exponent that lies within two standard errors of a small fraction, and send the candidate down the existing fit and holdout path; use the log-design rank for real identifiability in `frontier.ts`. Files: `probe/generator.ts`, `probe/structure.ts`, `probe/frontier.ts`. Test: a synthetic `y = π₁^{1/2} π₂^{-1}` family is recovered with exponents and intervals, and a null family is not. Gain: the probe goes from monomial-or-nothing to exponent discovery with uncertainties.

**9. Standard errors on fitted coefficients.** The prefactor fit and the study's basis coefficients print a number with no uncertainty. `ols` on the (σ-scaled) design gives the standard error; the study prints `c₁ = 0.0674 ± …` and the corpus states agreement in σ. Files: `probe/fit.ts`, `probe/study.ts`, `probe/corpus.ts`. Test: the large-amplitude control's θ² coefficient against the series `1/16` in units of its σ. Gain: the NOTES control becomes quantitative.

**10. Structure probes as tests.** The changepoint score is `|μ_L − μ_R|` against an absolute 0.5 and conservation is `|ΔQ/Δt| < 1e-6`, both in the units of `y`. Use a Welch t-test (MathTS `studentTTest`, direct tail) at the best split with a seeded permutation null for the max over splits, and a slope test (`linearRegression`'s standard error) for conservation. Files: `probe/structure.ts`. Test: a scale-free synthetic signal and its scaled copy get the same verdict. Gain: thresholds that do not depend on the scale.

**11. A graded magnitude gate.** The funnel's magnitude falsifier is a hard `ordersApart > 3` on values "good to about one order". With a σ in decades per representative value (default one decade), report `p = 2Φ(−orders/√(σ²+σ²))` beside the verdict, through MathTS `normalCDF` of the negative argument (never `1 − cdf`). Files: `composition/discovery.ts`, `representative-values.ts`. Test: 2.9 decades apart is no longer a clean pass; the verdict counts are unchanged. Gain: a calibrated strength, not a credibility score.

**12. The effective σ through one uncertainty path.** The study's `effectiveSigma` hand-codes central differences; MathTS `propagateUncertainty` is already the GUM path in `composition/uncertainty.ts`. Route the study through it, keep the step floor for a zero input, and give the three central-difference rules in `bridges/sensitivity.ts`, `diff/bridge-gradient.ts` and the study one owner with the representable-step denominator. Test: the χ² values on the fixtures move only in the last digits; the public gradient's step is relative to a small SI value. Gain: one derivative rule, and the public gradient no longer takes a 10¹³× step on a charge.

**13. Axis gates earn their place by evidence.** An axis is gated when it fires at least once. Test it instead: Fisher's exact test (MathTS `fisherExact`) of axis-fires against the independent magnitude falsifier over the candidates where both ran. Files: `composition/axis-audit.ts`. Test: a synthetic axis that fires at random is not earned; the scale axis is. Gain: the gate is evidence rather than activity.

**14. Convergence order from three resolutions.** A numeric witness reports one coarse-to-fine ratio. With three or more resolutions, MathTS `linearRegression` of `log err` on `log h` gives an observed order with a standard error, reported beside the ratio and never asserted. Files: `atlas/witness-numeric.ts`. Test: a known O(h²) witness reports 2 ± small; a noisy one reports a wide interval. Gain: the runner says how sure it is of the order.

## C. Independent second methods

**15. The exact pendulum.** `θ(t) = 2 asin(k sn(K − ω₀t, k²))` from MathTS `jacobiSN` and `ellipticK` checks the Fourier-series premise of the position translation densely (every θ₀, every phase) at 1e-13, where today six points are checked against RK4 at 1e-9; the series coefficients come from the FFT of one period. Files: `atlas/oscillators/position-translation.ts` and its tests. Test: series against the elliptic solution over a grid; RK4 stays the third method. Gain: a dense, independent check of a declared translation.

**16. A numeric probe that can refute a symbolic witness.** The CAS witnesses assert "not checked" when the simplifier fails, which is the weaker guarantee NOTES records. Evaluate both sides at seeded random points through `expr-eval` and refute on disagreement; report it as its own result kind, never merged into the CAS count. Files: `atlas/witness-symbolic.ts`, `witness-specs.ts`, the negative controls. Test: the four true claims agree to 1e-16; a wrong dictionary is refuted. Gain: the symbolic controls become refutable.

**17. Exact propagators and spectra beside the integrators.** The massless-limit witness and the Langevin ratio integrate linear constant-coefficient systems; the matrix exponential (`matrixExpm`) is their exact propagator. The chain-wave frequency is an eigenvalue of a circulant (`eigvals`, or the FFT of its first column). The damped-resonator linewidth has a sampled spectrum (`goertzel`) as a second method, which also measures the dropped pole at low Q. Files: `atlas/oscillators/limit-witnesses.ts`, `atlas/diffusion/numerics.ts`, `cases/damped-resonator.ts`, each as a test-side check. Test: agreement at the recorded precision. Gain: each integrator has an exact partner.

**18. Exact derivatives for the curvature pipeline, test-side.** The Christoffels of the built-in metrics are finite differences of finite differences, with a Bianchi noise floor near 1e-7. Write the metric components as expressions and apply MathTS symbolic `derivative` twice; compare Γ, ∂Γ and the Bianchi residual at machine precision. Files: tests under `tests/numerical/`. Test: the FD pipeline against the exact one on Schwarzschild and FLRW. Gain: a convergence-limited check becomes a machine-precision one.

## D. Truer maps

**19. Map structure from graph algorithms.** `upt map` shows components and "hubs" (quantities in two or more edges) and draws the 84-law core as one blob. Use MathTS `connectedComponents` as the one component owner (three union-find copies today), `louvainCommunities` for sub-clusters of the core (presented as layout, never physics), `betweennessCentrality` on demand for hub and cut-quantity ranking, and `stronglyConnectedComponents` on the directed graph for derivation cycles, where over-determination lives. Files: `composition/bridge-analysis.ts`, `graph-viz*.ts`, `cli/commands/map.ts`. Test: component ids unchanged; the core splits into named communities; a known cycle is listed. Gain: a readable core and a principled hub list.

**20. Link prediction with honest intervals, labelled post hoc.** The recorded negative result (typed neighbourhood 0.70 versus text 0.80 on twenty trials) stands and is not re-run for a better answer. Add paths beyond length two (a Katz index through `inv`) as a labelled post-hoc predictor, and give every reported recall and MRR a bootstrap or permutation interval (seeded). Files: `atlas/link-prediction.ts`, `composition/bridge-prediction.ts`. Test: the recorded numbers are reproduced with intervals; the post-hoc line is marked as such. Gain: the next reader sees how wide twenty trials are.

## What this note does not propose

A coincidence generator of any kind (the cross-cluster π-group mode was tested and rejected); tightening or loosening the funnel's verdicts; reproducing the lattice solvers through a PDE library (the dictionary under test must not enter the solver); a sparse-tensor rewrite of the catalog.
